# Professional Portfolio
Back to [[SYSA1011]]

> [!NOTE]
> **Overview**
> Built progressively through the semester; all components are due in the final portfolio (**Week 11** checkpoint/submission). Mostly **completion-based, reflective and developmental**. AI may help with wording/organization, but everything must reflect **your own** skills and reasoning - you must be able to explain it.

## Components
### 1. Skills & Career Inventory (Module 2)
* **Career interests** - which IT areas (sysadmin, networking, security, cloud, ops, dev, support, service delivery, PM, leadership, BA, customer success, training)? What role after graduation and why? One company/industry you'd like to work for and why
* **Skills inventory** - technical skills, transferable skills, strengths you bring
* **Growth** - skills to improve, what might be challenging in IT, what professionalism means to you

### 2. Resume Draft (Module 3)
* One page, entry-level IT
* **Required:** contact info, education, technical skills, work/volunteer experience, projects/labs/academic experience
* Optional: summary, certifications, awards/leadership
* No IT job needed - labs, projects, customer service, teamwork, volunteering count
* Tailor to postings for **ATS** - see [[Resume Workshop and Job Posting Analysis]]

### 3. LinkedIn & Digital Professionalism (Module 5)
* Profile with photo, headline, education, career interests, skills, About section, **custom URL**
* Reflection: why digital professionalism matters, how online presence affects hiring, what you improved
* Submit a link or screenshots/PDF (profile can be private) - see [[LinkedIn and Digital Professionalism]]

### 4. Professional Communication & AI Critique (Module 7)
* Scenario: Jordan's newly provisioned laptop (projector, Windows Hello, updates) before a presentation tomorrow
1. AI-generated user response
2. Critique it (strengths, weaknesses, unrealistic parts)
3. Revised professional response (tone, clarity, empathy, realism)
4. Internal **S-I-R** message to one audience (technician, supervisor, deployment team or service desk lead) + why you chose them and what you included/excluded
5. Reflection: why human judgment still matters
* See [[Communication and Active Listening]]

### 5. Ethics & Workplace Reflection (Module 6)
* **Part 1:** one workplace scenario - concerns, who's affected (trust, productivity, safety, privacy, fairness), why someone might minimize it, what a professional would do
* **Part 2:** a recent **news** example (AI misuse, security, privacy, misconduct, conflict of interest) with an **APA citation** - why it keeps happening, responsibilities, lesson learned

### 6. Technical Professionalism Artifact (Module 9)
* One professional technical document: troubleshooting summary, ticket/escalation note, **KB article**, setup guide, FAQ, lab documentation, deployment notes...
* Can be adapted from SYSA/NETW labs (these notes are a good source)
* Reflection: why documentation matters, what makes technical communication hard, technical vs non-technical audiences

### 7. Career Target & Job Posting Analysis (Module 10)
* **Two** current entry-level postings + **one aspirational** role
* Part 1: repeated skills, surprises, what you're ready for, what to improve
* Part 2: why the aspirational role, what's required, what to develop, steps toward it
* Include links/screenshots; APA for citations - see [[Career Strategy and Job Posting Analysis]]
