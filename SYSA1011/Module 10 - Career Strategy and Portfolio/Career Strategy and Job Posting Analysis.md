# Career Strategy and Job Posting Analysis
Back to [[SYSA1011]] · Prev: [[Workplace Respect and Professional Awareness]]

> [!quote] Career strategy is **evidence-building**.

## Start with evidence, not guesses
* Job postings, skills inventories, resume feedback, LinkedIn, labs/projects, portfolio artifacts, feedback
* Connect **where you are now** to **where you want to go next**

## The job posting is a career map
* What the role really does, which skills repeat across the industry, which technologies matter, entry-level expectations, valued behaviours, your gaps
* Read postings as **career information**, not just application instructions

## Three horizons
| Horizon | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Realistic** | Apply soon/after graduation | Help Desk Technician, Service Desk Analyst, Desktop Support, IT Support Specialist, Junior Technical Support Analyst |
| **Stretch** | Needs more experience/evidence/credentials | Junior Sysadmin, Network Support Analyst, Cloud Support Associate, Cybersecurity Analyst, IT Project Coordinator |
| **Longer-term** | Grow toward over several years | Systems Engineer, Cloud Engineer, Security Specialist, IT Manager, Project Manager |
* You don't need the whole career figured out - just a **realistic next step**

## Ladders, lattices and T-shaped skills
* **Ladder:** Help Desk → Junior Admin → Systems Admin → Senior Admin
* **Lattice:** Help Desk → Service Delivery → Project Coordination → IT Leadership (sideways/diagonal)
* **T-shaped:** broad foundation (OS, networking, security basics, troubleshooting, communication, documentation) + a developing specialty (cloud, security, sysadmin, databases, service delivery, project coordination)
	* *Breadth helps you start. Depth helps you grow.*

## Job postings aren't perfect
* Often written by HR, copied from old templates, overloaded with nice-to-haves, vague, unrealistic about entry-level experience
* **Read carefully - don't self-screen out**

### Anatomy of a posting
Title · organization/department · daily duties · **required** vs **preferred** qualifications · technical skills · professional skills · certifications · experience level · **work conditions** (remote/hybrid/on-site, shifts, on-call, travel)

| Required | Preferred |
| --- | --- |
| Strongest expectations - education, experience, technical skills, legal requirements | Helps you stand out; may be negotiable or developable |
* Before skipping: which requirements are truly essential? Which gaps can I explain or develop? What evidence do I have?

## Three kinds of proof
* **Technical skills** - tools, platforms, troubleshooting, configuration
* **Professional skills** - communication, teamwork, reliability, documentation, customer service
* **Evidence** - labs, projects, work, portfolio artifacts, tickets, screenshots, reflections

| Weak claim | Stronger evidence |
| --- | --- |
| "I have strong communication skills." | "I revised a workplace email, explained tone choices, and critiqued an AI-generated response for professionalism." |
| "I know technical troubleshooting." | "I documented a technical issue, listed steps attempted, included screenshots, and explained the final outcome." |

## Worked example - Junior IT Support Analyst
* **Technical:** Windows, Active Directory, Microsoft 365, networking basics, ticketing systems
* **Professional:** communication, customer service, prioritization, documentation, escalation judgment
* **Preferred:** previous help desk experience
* **Portfolio evidence:** resume draft, technical professionalism artifact, communication & AI critique, scenario reflections
* Likely interview question: *"Walk me through how you'd handle a ticket you couldn't resolve."*

## Gap analysis without panic
> A gap is not a failure. **A gap is information.**
* What do I have? What can I prove? What am I missing? Which gap matters most? How do I build evidence over the next month/semester/year?

| Weak next step | Specific next step |
| --- | --- |
| "I should learn cloud." | "Over the next month I'll complete one Azure Fundamentals module, take notes, and add a reflection to my portfolio." |
| "I need better experience." | "I'll document one lab or troubleshooting task using issue, steps, evidence, result, next steps." |
