# Workplace Scenario Practice
Back to [[SYSA1011]] · Prev: [[Communication and Active Listening]] · Next: [[Workplace Respect and Professional Awareness]]

> [!NOTE]
> Practice for [[Assessment 2 - Professional Workplace Simulation Lab]]. Discuss in groups; in the assessment every response is written **individually**, in **point form**.

## Four questions for every scenario
1. **Primary issue** - what's the main concern?
2. **Policy or expectation** - which policy, professional expectation or ethical concern applies?
3. **Action** - most appropriate professional response?
4. **Follow-up** - is communication, escalation or follow-up needed? Why?

## 1 - Curiosity about a colleague's files
*Fixing an HR coworker's slow laptop with admin access; a "Performance Reviews 2026" folder is on the desktop. Another tech: "Just take a quick look."*
* **Issue:** privacy/confidentiality - browsing is outside the repair; peer pressure
* **Policy:** access only what the task needs; admin access is for the assigned work; trust placed in IT
* **Action:** don't open it; finish the repair. "Nobody will know" doesn't change what's right
* **Follow-up:** "I'm not comfortable opening that - we only need to fix the performance." If the colleague opens it or it repeats → document (what, when, device) and tell your supervisor

## 2 - Skipping the change process
*Senior tech wants to delete logs and restart a service on a production server during business hours, no change approval: "It's a five-minute job."*
* **Issue:** bypassing change management on production; experience vs process; power difference
* **Policy:** change management applies to production; approved + recorded changes
* **Action:** don't join in; raise it respectfully; ask about an **expedited/emergency change** - the disk isn't full yet, there's time
* **Follow-up:** "Could we submit this as an expedited change? I can help write it up." If they proceed and risk is clear → inform supervisor/change owner, document

## 3 - Taking work home the easy way
*Teammate emails spreadsheets of customer contact details to their personal email instead of using the VPN: "I'll delete them when I'm done."*
* **Issue:** customer data sent outside approved systems; convenience over secure access
* **Policy:** data handling + remote access policies; duty to protect customer info
* **Action:** talk privately, focus on behaviour/impact, offer VPN help
* **Follow-up:** deleting later doesn't undo it - a supervisor/privacy contact must know. Encourage them to report; if not, you report factually

## 4 - Owning a mistake
*You accidentally disabled a service account a nightly backup uses, re-enabled it minutes later. Coworker: "If you say something you'll just get in trouble."*
* **Issue:** accountability/honesty; unknown whether the backup ran
* **Policy:** report incidents that may affect service; don't conceal mistakes
* **Action:** check the backup status now; write down what/when/how fixed
* **Follow-up:** tell your supervisor **today** (email/ticket), loop in the backup owner: *"I disabled the service account by mistake at 10:15 and re-enabled it at 10:20. I'm checking whether last night's backup ran and will update you."* An openly handled mistake is recoverable

## 5 - Credit for shared work
*You wrote most of an onboarding automation; your teammate presents it as theirs and gets praised. "Don't make this a big deal."*
* **Issue:** recognition/teamwork; possibly thoughtless rather than intentional; contributions undocumented
* **Policy:** professional integrity, accurate credit, respectful factual communication, fair reviews
* **Action:** don't react in the meeting; talk privately and specifically; **document contributions** from now on (tickets, commits, emails)
* **Follow-up:** "I wrote most of the scripts - can we present the next update together?" If it repeats → manager, with facts not accusations

## Key takeaways
* Same four steps every time: **identify, consider impact, determine action, communicate/escalate**
* Focus on **behaviour and impact**, not personality
* Decide whether to **handle directly, document, or escalate** - know when an issue is bigger than you
* **Disclose and own mistakes early** - transparency protects trust
* Rarely one perfect answer - strong responses are **reasoned, balanced, professional**
