# Conflict of Interest and Disclosure
Back to [[SYSA1011]] · Prev: [[Ethics in IT Decision Making]] · Next: [[LinkedIn and Digital Professionalism]]

* IT professionals influence **software purchases**, **hardware procurement**, **vendor relationships**, **hiring**, and **access to information** - organizations depend on **objective** decisions

## Definition
> A **conflict of interest** occurs when personal interests *may* interfere with professional responsibilities.
* The concern isn't always misconduct - it's whether the **decision process stays fair and trustworthy**

## Three types
| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Actual** | A direct conflict exists now | Hiring your nephew (you're on the panel); owning stock in a vendor under review |
| **Potential** | Could arise from current circumstances | Your spouse works for a *future* bidder |
| **Perceived** | Others may reasonably believe bias exists, even if it doesn't | You previously worked for the vendor |

## Credibility and perception
* A manager picks a vendor; staff later learn they **own shares** in it → trust drops **even if it was the best choice**
* Perception matters because it affects credibility

## Disclosure
* **Informing the appropriate people** about relationships, interests or circumstances that could influence decisions
* Protects **employees**, protects **organizations**, **maintains trust**, **avoids disputes**
* **Disclosure ≠ wrongdoing** - it shows professionalism, transparency, accountability and good judgment

### Ask yourself before a decision
1. Would I **benefit personally**?
2. Do I have a **financial interest**?
3. Is a **personal relationship** involved?
4. Could someone **question my objectivity**?

→ Any "yes" = consider disclosure (and possibly stepping back from the decision)

## Scenarios
* **Your brother works for a vendor** you think is genuinely best → conflict (actual/perceived) - **disclose** and let others weigh in
* **Vendor gifts** (Oilers tickets, dinner, conference travel) **during an evaluation** → follow the gift policy; timing matters; disclose; often decline
* **Recommending your own side business** to your employer → the undisclosed financial interest is the problem, even if your business is the best option

## Escalation and reporting
* Escalate when: **policy may be violated**, **organizational risk exists**, **fairness could be questioned**, or **you lack authority** to resolve it
* Other reportable concerns: **fraud**, **data misuse**, **security concerns**, **policy violations**, **serious misconduct** - use the org's procedures (chain of command, HR, hotlines)

### Raising concerns professionally
1. **Gather facts** - specific, documented evidence
2. **Avoid assumptions** - observed behaviour, not intent
3. **Follow procedures** - established channels
4. **Document observations** - dates, events, communications
5. **Remain objective** - neutral language

## Practice
* A committee member pushing a ticketing vendor used to work there and is still close friends with its staff → **perceived/potential** conflict → they should disclose; if they haven't, raise it with the committee chair
* A committee member's close family member works at a vendor, **never disclosed**, no evidence of misconduct → **absence of misconduct doesn't remove the need for disclosure**

## Workplace framework (Assessment 2)
1. **Identify the issue**
2. **Consider impact**
3. **Determine action**
4. **Communicate** - is communication or escalation required?

## Key takeaways
1. Conflicts may be **actual, potential, or perceived**
2. **Disclosure protects** employees and organizations
3. Professional judgment often involves **ambiguity**
4. **Trust and credibility** matter
5. **Communication and escalation** are workplace skills
