# Workplace Respect and Professional Awareness
Back to [[SYSA1011]] · Prev: [[Workplace Scenario Practice]] · Next: [[Career Strategy and Job Posting Analysis]]

* Working effectively with different people **is** IT professionalism - practical behaviour, not perfection

## Respect is operational
Respect affects whether people **ask for help**, **report mistakes**, **trust technical advice**, **follow security practices**, and **work well with the team**.
> **Respect protects trust.** In IT, respect is part of service quality.

## Cultural competency (practically)
* Working respectfully with people of different **backgrounds, communication styles, work habits, experiences, expectations, comfort with technology**
* Not becoming an expert in every culture:
	* Be aware of communication preferences
	* **Don't assume technical ability**
	* Adapt without losing professionalism
	* **Ask rather than assume**
* You'll work with new staff and senior leaders, technical and non-technical users, local and remote teams, vendors/contractors, people under stress

## Assumptions create risk
* "They're not paying attention." · "They should already know this." · "They're being difficult." · "They're not a good fit." · "They don't care."
* **Assumption → Action → Impact** - assumptions lead to poor decisions and disrespect

### The professional pause
1. What am I **assuming**?
2. What **else** could explain this?
3. **Who** could be affected by my response?
4. What would a **professional** response look like?

## Scenarios
| Scenario | Assumption / risk | Professional move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Support chat: *"I already explained MFA twice. Some people just shouldn't be allowed to work remotely."* | User is incapable; contempt leaks into service | Explain another way, send written steps, suggest clearer docs |
| Quiet coworker left out of decisions because "they probably don't care" | Quiet = disengaged | Ask directly, offer async/written input, check in privately |
| Coworker jokes about a user's **accent** in a private IT chat | "It's private" | **Private workplace spaces are still workplace spaces** - "Let's keep the chat professional." Escalate if it continues |

## Respectful correction phrases
* "Let's focus on the issue, not the person."
* "I don't think we have enough information to assume that."
* "Let's keep the chat professional."
* "Can we document the issue and decide the next step?"
* "I can explain that another way."

## Respectful documentation
| ❌ Unprofessional | ✅ Professional |
| --- | --- |
| "User does not understand basic login steps." | "User was unable to complete MFA login after password reset. Walked through verification steps and confirmed successful sign-in." |
| "User keeps messing this up." | "Repeated issue reported. Recommend follow-up training or clearer setup instructions." |

## Respectful escalation
* **Factual, timely, respectful, impact-focused, free of blame/assumptions**
* Include: **what happened**, **who is affected**, **what has been tried**, **what support is needed**

## In interviews
* "Tell me about a time you worked with someone difficult." · "How would you handle a frustrated user?" · "What if a coworker made an inappropriate comment?" · "How do you communicate with non-technical users?"
* Strong answers show **respect, self-control, judgment, communication**

### Quick practice - user asks the same question three times
* A "I already explained this earlier." ❌
* B "This is pretty simple once you get used to it." ❌
* **C "No problem. Let's try it another way and I'll send you the steps after."** ✅
* D "You may want to ask your manager for more training." ❌

## Professional respect means
Pausing before assuming · focusing on the issue, not the person · communicating clearly and calmly · documenting without blame · protecting workplace trust
