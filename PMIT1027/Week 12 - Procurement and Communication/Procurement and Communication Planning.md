# Procurement
* **PMBOK** (Project Management Body of Knowledge) - the formal industry standard, theory-heavy
	* PMBOK: *"processes necessary to purchase or acquire products, services, or results needed from outside the project team"*
	* Plain English: **how your project buys stuff or gets outside help - without messing up the paperwork**

## What procurement is
1. Identify what the project needs
2. **Build or buy?**
3. Get quotes / approvals
4. Purchase
5. Make sure it arrives **on time and as expected**

* IT procurement: hardware (laptops, servers, switches), **software licences**, cloud subscriptions, consultants, cables/peripherals, printing/supplies
* Links to the **budget** (bigger purchases need more approvals) and affects schedule, risk and what you can deliver

## Why not just buy it at Best Buy on a credit card?
* **Control costs** (no impulse buys) · **ensure quality** (meets requirements) · **manage risk** (no unsupported/fake/incompatible tech) · **accountability** (who approved what) · **protect the org** (warranty, returns, support, vendor compliance)

## Procurement failures
| Case | What went wrong |
| --- | --- |
| **Target Canada** | Rushed supply-chain systems, poor vendor selection, untested tech, bad inventory data → stock late/wrong/missing |
| **Nike i2** | Supply-chain system not ready, unclear rushed requirements, poor integration → forecasting errors, **$100M+** lost sales |
| **Marriott / Starwood** | Acquisition without **security due diligence** - an existing breach exposed up to **339 million** guests |

## Thresholds and methods
| Example threshold | Process |
| --- | --- |
| Under $1,000 | Direct purchase |
| $1,000-$5,000 | Two quotes |
| Over $10,000 | Formal RFQ/RFP + approval |

| Method | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct buy** | Simple, low-cost, low-risk - fastest |
| **RFQ** (Request for Quote) | You know exactly what you need - compare prices/vendors |
| **RFP** (Request for Proposal) | Complex needs - vendors propose **solutions**, formal evaluation |
| **Internal sourcing** | Existing equipment/licences/shared resources |
| **Approved vendor list** | Org requires pre-approved suppliers |

* Public example: [Alberta Purchasing Connection](https://purchasing.alberta.ca/search) - government tenders/RFPs
* Risks: vendor delays/backorders, wrong/incompatible products, overruns, no warranty/support, shipping/customs

# Communication planning
* One-way drawing activity: one person describes, everyone draws → shows assumptions and breakdowns
* > Technical issues happen - **communication problems turn them into project failures**

## A communication plan answers
* **Stakeholders** - who needs information?
* **Information needs** - what do they need to know?
* **Frequency** - when / how often?
* **Method** - how is it delivered?
* **Owner** - who's responsible for sending it?

| Communication | Owner | Stakeholders | Frequency | Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Status update | PM | Sponsor, team | Weekly | MS Project report + email |
| Risk review | PM | Client, safety manager | Bi-weekly | Teams meeting |
| Scope review | PM | Sponsor | At each milestone | Meeting + email |
| Stakeholder review | Sponsor | Executives | Monthly | Email |
| Team meeting | Team lead | Team | Twice weekly | In person |

## Link to RACI
* **R** provide status info · **A** owns formal updates · **C** get updates **before** decisions · **I** get updates **after** decisions

## Communication risks
* Inconsistent/infrequent updates, unclear/overly technical messages, stakeholders left out, nobody owns updates, decisions not documented

### Fix this plan
> ❌ *"We'll communicate whenever something important happens. Everyone will send updates. Meetings will happen when we feel like it. Probably email?"*
>
> ✅ *"The PM sends a status update to the sponsor and team every Friday by email; the team meets Tuesdays and Thursdays on Teams; issues are escalated to the PM within 24 hours."*

## Case - Hawaii false missile alert (2018)
* Newly deployed alert system; unclear interface → an operator sent a **real** alert instead of a test
* No plan for **verifying** alerts, **escalating** mistakes, or **correcting** false messages → **38 minutes** to issue a correction
* Fixes: two-person verification, predefined correction channel, clear roles (communication matrix + RACI)

## Common mistakes
* "We will communicate as needed" · unclear ownership ("everyone sends updates") · forgotten stakeholders · no frequency · mixing up method vs purpose · never updated
