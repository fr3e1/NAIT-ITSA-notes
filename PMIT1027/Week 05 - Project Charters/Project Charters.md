# Project Charters
Back to [[PMIT1027]] · Prev: [[The Project Pitch]] · Next: [[Executive Summaries]]

## Why charters matter
* **Formal authorization** - the official "permission slip" to begin (like a field trip form: no slip, no trip)
* **Alignment tool** - sponsor, PM and team agree on purpose, scope and limits
* **Boundary setter** - prevents scope creep by defining in/out at a high level
* **Communication starter** - a common reference point before detailed planning

## Charter vs plan
| Project charter | Project plan |
| --- | --- |
| High-level **authorization** | Detailed **roadmap** and tasks |
| *Permission to drive* | *Google Maps directions* |
| Comes **first** (Initiation) | Comes later (Planning) |

## Sections of a charter
| Section | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Purpose / description** | *Why* are we doing this? Connect organizational goals to the project |
| **Objectives & success criteria** | **SMART** objectives; success criteria answer "how do we know we're done?" |
| **Stakeholders & roles** | People/groups impacted - sponsor, manager, support staff, users. Identify early to avoid surprises |
| **Deliverables & milestones** | Deliverables = tangible outputs; milestones = progress checkpoints |
| **Costs & revenues** | **High level only** - not a full budget; revenue sources (tickets, sponsorships, grants) |
| **Risks** | What could go wrong (supplier delays, tech failures, low turnout) - **at least 10** in the assignment |
| **Assumptions & constraints** | |
| **Executive summary** | 1-3 paragraphs of big-picture value - often the **only part executives read** (see [[Executive Summaries]]) |

### SMART objectives
* **S**pecific · **M**easurable · **A**chievable · **R**elevant · **T**ime-bound
* e.g. *"Migrate 50 users to Microsoft 365 by December 15"*

## Case study - Phoenix Pay (Government of Canada, 2016)
* Replaced 40+ payroll systems with one IBM-built platform
* **300,000+ federal employees** hit by pay errors
* Auditor General: *"inadequate governance, unclear accountability, and lack of risk assessment"* → effectively **no strong charter**
* Discussion: which section (purpose, stakeholders, risks, success criteria) was most critical to get right?
