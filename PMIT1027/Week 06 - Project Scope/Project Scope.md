# Project Scope
Back to [[PMIT1027]] · Prev: [[Executive Summaries]] · Next: [[Work Breakdown Structure]]

## Why scope matters
* Defines what work **will** and **won't** be done
* Foundation for scheduling and resource planning
* Prevents assumptions and disagreements

## Charter vs scope statement
| Project charter | Scope statement |
| --- | --- |
| The **why** - purpose, justification, goals | The **what** - boundaries, deliverables, success criteria |
| Written **before** full planning | Written **once approved** and details are known |
| | Expands deliverables with **inclusions and exclusions** |

## Components of a strong scope statement
1. **Project context / summary** - 2-3 sentences on what and why; reference the approved charter
2. **Deliverables summary**
3. **Scope definition - in / out**
4. **Dependencies**
5. **Acceptance criteria**
6. **Scope creep prevention plan**

### In scope
* Exactly what's included; **specific, measurable, outcome-focused**
* e.g. "Design a survey to gather participant feedback", "Present findings in a written report and PowerPoint"

### Out of scope
* Prevents misunderstanding and creep; aligns expectations
* e.g. in-person follow-up interviews, publishing in a journal, translating the survey

| ❌ Weak | ✅ Strong |
| --- | --- |
| Do a survey | Design a short online survey to gather feedback from **at least 50 students** |
| Make a PowerPoint | Create a PowerPoint presentation **summarizing survey results** |

*Activity:* one-day esports tournament at NAIT (student teams, small prize, refreshments; ends when results are shared) - what's in vs out?

### Dependencies
* What the project relies on: other teams, approvals, systems, resources → surfaces risks early
* e.g. domain name registered; server passes a vulnerability assessment
* Esports chain: **book lab** → confirm date → print posters → set up registration → hold event

### Acceptance criteria
* How you'll know it's **complete and successful** - e.g. portal passes user testing with no critical defects; the sponsor approves

## Scope creep
* **Uncontrolled expansion of work without adjusting time, cost or resources**
* Causes: unclear boundaries, stakeholder "add-ons", poor change control
* **Prevention plan:** document all requests → discuss in regular meetings → approve only if aligned with objectives and deadlines (formal [[Change and Risk Management|change control]])

## Case study - Berlin Brandenburg (BER) Airport
* Years late and massively over budget from expanding, unclear scope; who suffered most - engineers, management, taxpayers, travellers? Did politics play a role?
